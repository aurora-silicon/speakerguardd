# ControlFreak slow-release control path

This is the clean behavioural specification for the slow-gain coefficient
selector. It is stated independently of any installed implementation.

For each audio frame, sum the post-input-gain, post-high-pass and
post-saturation channels in Float32. Feed that signed linked value to a
streaming 4:1 decimator. The decimator is a 31-tap symmetric Float32 FIR and
emits at phases 0, 4, 8, ... across process-call boundaries. Its public clean
design is a least-squares low pass generated by SciPy `signal.firls` with 31
taps, bands `[0, 0.0755, 0.125, 0.5]`, desired values `[1, 1, 0, 0]`, weights
`[1, 1500]`, and `fs=1`. The checked-in taps are the Float32 form of that
reproducible design, not a table copied from an installed unit.

Convert every decimated sample to a quadrature magnitude with two cascaded
Float32 biquads per branch. The topology and base coefficients are the public
Favreau Hilbert pair distributed with Pure Data's `hilbert~`; the numerator
normalisation is fixed by public-AudioUnit DC and step measurements. Each
biquad is transposed direct-form II. Then select every tenth magnitude with a
phase that persists across process calls.

Square the selected magnitude in Float32. Keep a 30 ms ring at `fs / 40`,
but accumulate its replacement sum in Float64. The selector input is
`Float32(sum / window + 1e-10)`. A value more than 5 dB above the anchor sets
the anchor and adds `ceil(fs / 5000)` control frames of fast-release budget; a
value more than 5 dB below the anchor only moves the anchor down. Consume one
budget frame per control update. A nonzero budget selects the 270 ms
audio-rate coefficient; zero selects the 10 s coefficient.

Hold each selected coefficient through the matching 10x and 4x return phases.
In streaming form this is the same as retaining the last selector result until
the next 40:1 control update. The phase is never restarted at a render-call
boundary. Align a fast-to-slow edge to the start of the look-ahead interval:
equivalently, restore the slow state at that edge by undoing the preceding
`L` fast-pole moves, where `L` is the look-ahead in frames. The normalised
slow target is per-frame, without the look-ahead
minimum used by the final fast-gain stage. Slow gain takes a lower target
immediately; an upward move applies the retained coefficient once per audio
frame. The final combined target retains the configured hold and look-ahead
minimum before its sine-window reconstruction.

The clean primitives come from licensed public implementations:

- Pure Data `hilbert~` (BSD-3-Clause project):
  <https://github.com/pure-data/pure-data/blob/master/extra/hilbert~.pd>
- SciPy `signal.firls` (BSD-3-Clause project):
  <https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.firls.html>

Parity fixtures use only synthetic signals and measurements made through the
public AudioUnit interface. Binary-analysis working notes and copied data are
not part of this repository.
